U-3DED Structure Series

SHELXL refinements from a series of 3D ED datasets: bond lengths, angles, displacement parameters and atomic shifts against pump–probe delay. The baseline is the mean of every selected dataset at negative delay. Atoms are matched by label and moved onto the symmetry copy nearest the reference structure.

Bonds and angles

Pick a central atom, then add bonds from its neighbour list, or tick two neighbours to add the angle between them. Each distance is defined once in the reference structure, with its symmetry operator, and then measured the same way in every dataset. Error bars come from the CIF geometry list where SHELXL wrote one (full covariance). Otherwise they are propagated from the coordinate and cell esds without covariance.

Displacement parameters

Ueq comes from the CIF where there is one, otherwise it is computed from the anisotropic Uij in the .res. Umax and Umin are the largest and smallest principal mean-square displacements. Isotropic atoms show Uiso for every choice.

Atoms

ADP ellipses

The outline of each atom’s displacement ellipsoid projected onto a lattice plane, one ellipse per selected dataset, all centred on the origin. The outline is the projection of the ellipsoid that holds the chosen probability, so a 50 % ellipse here matches the ellipsoid ORTEP would draw. In a plane whose two axes are not at 90° (the ac plane of a monoclinic cell, for example), the vertical axis is the direction in that plane perpendicular to the first, marked with a prime.

Atomic shifts

How far each atom has moved from its baseline position. The baseline position is the mean fractional coordinate over the selected datasets at negative delay, or the reference structure if there are none. Shifts are converted to Å with the baseline cell, so a cell that expands with the atoms fixed in fractional coordinates gives no shift here. The components along a, b and c are Δx·a, Δy·b and Δz·c.

Notes

Which file wins

For each experiment, atoms, cell and esds come from the .cif if there is one, otherwise from the .res (cell esds from ZERR, coordinate esds are then zero, so propagated bond esds only carry the cell part). Refinement figures take the CIF value first, then the last cycle in the .lst, then the REM lines at the end of the .res. The .fcf is only used for the Fo² against Fc² plot and its own counts.

Matching atoms between datasets

Atoms are matched by label, ignoring case. Before anything is measured, every atom is moved to the symmetry copy (space-group operator plus lattice translation) that lies closest to the same label in the reference structure. A dataset where SHELXT happened to put V1 on a different but equivalent site therefore still measures the same bond. The table notes how many atoms were moved this way and which labels are missing.

Reference structure

The reference can be one dataset or the mean of every selected dataset before t0. The mean is the default when at least two datasets qualify. To build it, each of those structures is first moved onto the symmetry copies nearest one seed structure (a CIF if there is one). Coordinates, cell and Uij are then averaged, and the esd of the mean is taken from their scatter. Ticking or unticking a dataset before t0 changes the mean, and with it the neighbour list. Bonds that are already defined keep their symmetry operators.

“Load reference CIF” adds any CIF as the reference, for example a literature or database structure. It is not plotted as a dataset. Where your labels differ from the ones in that file, each unmatched atom is paired with the nearest site of the same element (within 1 Å, any symmetry copy) and renamed; the table lists these pairings. The CIF needs its symmetry operator list; a CIF with only a space-group symbol is read as P1.

Floating origin

In polar space groups the origin along the polar axis is not fixed, so a whole structure can slide between refinements. Tick “remove common shift” to subtract the mean shift of all non-hydrogen atoms in each dataset before plotting atomic shifts. Bond lengths and angles are unaffected either way.
