Room-temperature candidates for U-3DED

The list re-cut for an experiment that runs at ambient. Roughly a third of the original candidates depended on a phase that only exists below 250 K and are gone; several that were buried in the frontier tier move up, because room temperature is where they were always meant to work. One change matters more than any single sample choice — see below.

What room temperature changes

Read this before the candidate list

Losing the cryostat costs you three things: low-temperature ordered phases, roughly a factor of 3–10 in dose tolerance on soft materials, and the thermal ballast that was quietly absorbing your average laser power. The first is unavoidable. The second and third are manageable. And one thing gets easier.

Your temperature knob now only goes up — so use it

A heating holder reaching ~500 K costs a fraction of a cryo holder and reopens a surprising amount of this list: VO₂ at 340 K, TaS₂'s NC→IC transition at ~350 K, Ta₂NiSe₅ at 328 K, BaTiO₃ at 393 K, CsPbBr₃'s cubic phase at 403 K, superionic AgI at 420 K.

More important than the extra systems is what poising does to your signal. Sitting 5–15 K below a transition drops the photoinduced threshold fluence sharply, so you get a much larger structural response at a fluence the sample survives. On every borderline candidate here, that is the difference between a 1% effect fighting your drift and a 20% effect you can refine. It is the single highest-leverage change you can make to a room-temperature plan.

The catch, which is now your dominant systematic: poised near a transition, steady-state laser heating can push you across it without warning. Check the negative-delay lattice parameter against a genuine pump-off measurement at every working point.

The filter

Six gates, the last one new

A 4D dataset is a rotation series multiplied by a delay series — hours of acquisition where tilt and delay are scanned on different clocks. That cost sets the selection rules more than the physics does.

  1. Gate 01

    3D has to be load-bearing

    Either the answer is a refinable structure, or the signal has an out-of-plane component — satellite L-index, diffuse sheets, rods. A Debye–Waller drop on one ring does not qualify.

  2. Gate 02

    Reversible for 10⁷–10¹⁰ shots

    Fatigue or slow accumulation masquerades as delay dependence. Room temperature helps here — most states relax faster and fewer metastable phases latch — but it removes cryo's dose protection, so budget 3–10× less dose on organics, MOFs and perovskites.

  3. Gate 03

    Slower than ~3–5 ps

    A 2 ps instrument response erases coherent optical phonons. What survives: order-parameter relaxation, carrier–lattice thermalisation, acoustic strain, nucleation, orientational disordering.

  4. Gate 04

    Uniform excitation through thickness

    Pump absorption depth must exceed the crystal thickness or you refine a depth-averaged fiction. Oxides and semiconductors pumped near the gap are fine at 50–150 nm; metals give you a gradient.

  5. Gate 05

    Thin enough to be quasi-kinematic

    Under ~100 nm for light-element crystals, less for heavy ones — or budget for dynamical refinement. This is where your beam-tilt integration earns its keep.

  6. Gate 06 — new

    The ordered phase must exist at 295 K

    Or within reach of a heating holder above it. Anything whose interesting phase sits below room temperature is out, however good the physics — that is what the cut list above is.

Category A

Phase transformation

The category most disturbed by the room-temperature constraint, since so many photoinduced transitions were characterised in systems chosen for a convenient low Tc. What remains is the set whose transition sits above ambient — which means the heating holder is doing real work in every entry here.

Tier 1 build the method on these

Vanadium dioxide

VO₂ · M1 → R · Tc = 340 K
Superlattice collapse 295 K native · poise at 330 K Nanobeams Risk: low
Why it survives
The transition sits only 45 K above ambient, which is exactly the situation room-temperature work wants: the ordered phase is the room-temperature phase, and the transition is one heating step away.
Signal
V–V dimerisation superlattice reflections extinguish as the monoclinic cell relaxes to rutile. Strong, unambiguous, visible on a single frame.
Why 3D
M1, M2 and the proposed monoclinic-metallic phase differ in which subset of superlattice reflections survives, and those subsets are spread through reciprocal space. A decade of single-zone argument exists precisely because one zone axis cannot separate them.
RT tactic
Poise at 325–335 K. The photoinduced threshold falls steeply as you approach Tc, so you get a full transition at a fluence that does not crack the sample. Run a fluence series at two or three base temperatures — the threshold-versus-temperature curve is itself a result.
Sample
Hydrothermal nanobeams, 50–200 nm, dropcast on SiN or graphene. Free-standing beams relieve strain and survive far more cycles than a clamped film.
Verdict
Still the safest first real experiment, and now also the best-behaved sample for learning what your heating holder does to drift and eucentricity.
structural window

λ-Ti₃O₅

Ti₃O₅ · λ ↔ β photo-switch · room-temperature bistable
Large volume change 295 K native Nanocrystals Risk: medium
Why it moves up
It was in the frontier tier only because better-studied competitors existed at low temperature. Room-temperature bistability is its defining property, so under this constraint it becomes a first-choice sample rather than an also-ran.
Signal
Photoinduced metal ↔ semiconductor switching with roughly 7% volume change and a monoclinic cell change — one of the largest structural responses available at ambient.
Why 3D
The λ and β cells are both monoclinic and closely related; distinguishing them, and quantifying the intermediate, needs the reflection list rather than a few peak positions. With a 7% volume change you also get real leverage on the transient cell from a rotation series.
Design task
Bistable means it does not reset itself. Plan the reset before you scan — second-colour erase or a brief thermal excursion between delay series — and verify recovery with a repeated reference delay.
Verdict
The biggest structural signal on this page at ambient temperature. The reset scheme is the whole experiment design problem.

Fe(II)-triazole spin-crossover nanoparticles

[Fe(Htrz)₂(trz)](BF₄) · [Fe(NH₂trz)₃]X₂ · hysteresis near 295 K
Intensity redistribution Poise into the loop Nanorods / nanocubes Risk: high
Why 3D
Still the flagship argument for the whole method. Low-spin → high-spin elongates the Fe–N bonds by ~0.2 Å — a crystallographic result that needs hundreds of reflections and a real refinement to claim. Nobody has refined a transient molecular structure from a nanocrystal by electron diffraction.
What RT costs
The cryogenic LIESST state is gone, and with it the static ground-truth structure you would have refined at leisure and converged on. That was a large part of why this was low-risk before. It is now the highest-risk entry in Tier 1, kept because the payoff is unmatched.
RT tactic
Switch families. The triazole coordination polymers are the room-temperature spin-crossover materials — wide thermal hysteresis bracketing or just above ambient, and they are routinely made as 50–200 nm rods and cubes, which is exactly the specimen you want. Poise inside the hysteresis loop so a modest photoexcitation converts a large fraction rather than a few percent.
Timing
Intramolecular expansion ~1 ps, at or under your response. The lattice expansion and elastic step run 10–100 ps and sit squarely in your window. Room-temperature relaxation is ns–µs, which is good news: the sample resets itself between shots.
Verdict
Attempt it second, not first, and only once VO₂ has told you what your drift and dose budget actually are.

Category B

Structural disorder

Still your strongest category, and it survives the room-temperature cut better than expected — because disorder, unlike order, generally increases with temperature. Two of the three Tier 1 entries are unchanged; magnetite is the one real casualty, and the replacement is good rather than merely adequate.

Tier 1 build the method on these

1T-TaS₂, nearly-commensurate phase

NC phase spans ≈ 180–350 K · NC → IC at ≈ 350 K
CDW satellites, L-resolved 295 K native · poise at 340 K Exfoliated flakes Risk: medium
Why it survives
The nearly-commensurate phase is the room-temperature phase. You lose the hidden state, which needs the commensurate phase below 180 K, but you keep the material and the entire 3D argument.
Signal
At ambient the CDW forms hexagonal domains of √13 clusters separated by discommensurations, with a definite interlayer stacking — hundreds of satellites with a real L-dependence.
Why 3D
The cleanest argument for U-3DED that exists, and it is unchanged by temperature. The domain superstructure and its stacking live entirely in the out-of-plane direction. A [001] pattern — which is what nearly every published ultrafast study on this material shows — is structurally blind to it.
Two experiments
At ambient, photoinduced domain and discommensuration dynamics within the NC phase. Poised near 340 K, the photoinduced NC → IC transition itself, at a threshold low enough to be safely reversible.
Timing
CDW amplitude collapses under 1 ps, below your response. The stacking and domain rearrangement runs 1–100 ps — precisely the part you can see and a 2D experiment cannot interpret.
domain & stacking rearrangement

Rare-earth tritellurides

TbTe₃ (TCDW ≈ 336 K) · LaTe₃ (≈ 670 K) · DyTe₃
Unidirectional CDW satellites 295 K native Exfoliated flakes Risk: medium
Why it is new here
The direct replacement for TiSe₂ and blue bronze, both of which order well below ambient. The tritellurides carry their charge-density wave straight through room temperature, and LaTe₃ carries it to 670 K.
Signal
Incommensurate satellites along b* around every Bragg peak, with a well-studied amplitude mode. The heavier members develop a second, perpendicular CDW at low temperature — at ambient you have the clean unidirectional case.
Why 3D
Satellite intensity and its L-dependence encode the interlayer CDW correlation — the same argument as TaS₂ but in a simpler, better-characterised system, which makes it the better first CDW experiment even though TaS₂ is the more famous one.
Handling
Air-sensitive: tellurides oxidise. Exfoliate and load under inert atmosphere, or use your transfer holder without cooling it.
Verdict
Well-known ultrafast behaviour to benchmark against, and a genuinely new 3D question. Strong room-temperature substitute.
CDW recovery

Halide perovskite tilt disorder

CsPbBr₃ (Pnma at 295 K) · MAPbI₃ (tetragonal at 295 K)
Diffuse sheets & rods 295 K is the relevant condition Colloidal NCs Risk: high
Why 3D
Correlated dynamic tilting produces diffuse sheets and rods with a specific orientation in reciprocal space, and the correlation length is direction-dependent — a single planar cut cannot recover it. Mapping the full 3D diffuse volume against delay answers the field's live question: does photoexcitation locally order the tilt pattern or melt it, and over what distance?
RT is a feature
These materials are used at ambient. A cryogenic measurement answers a question about a phase no solar cell ever sees; the room-temperature phase is the one the field actually cares about.
RT is also the cost
Cryo cooling was buying most of your damage headroom on this sample. Without it, dose is the binding constraint, not counting statistics. Pilot a dose–damage curve on one crystallite before designing the scan, and plan to merge across many crystals.
Watch for
Ion migration and halide segregation under prolonged pumping look exactly like real dynamics, and both are faster at ambient. Re-measure a negative-delay reference at every tilt.

Category C

Lattice dynamics

Almost untouched by the room-temperature constraint — every Tier 1 entry was already ambient-native. Only the cryogenic frontier items drop out. Combined with the fact that a 2 ps response rules out counting optical-phonon oscillations but not phonon populations, this is now clearly your lowest-risk category.

Tier 1 build the method on these

Si, Ge, graphite, black phosphorus

momentum-resolved electron–phonon coupling by 3D thermal diffuse scattering
Diffuse across full BZ 295 K native Membranes / flakes Risk: very low
Why 3D
Ultrafast electron diffuse scattering conventionally measures one plane and models the rest. A rotation series gives the entire Brillouin zone volume, so the comparison against ab initio electron–phonon matrix elements no longer rests on an assumption about which plane you happened to sample.
RT impact
None, and slightly positive: the ambient thermal diffuse background is stronger, which makes the pump-induced change easier to normalise against a well-populated baseline.
Timing
Electron–phonon energy transfer 0.5–20 ps; anharmonic phonon–phonon decay 10–500 ps. Both inside your window and both slower than your response.
Sample
Commercial Si and Ge membranes; exfoliated graphite and black phosphorus. Non-fatiguing, stable indefinitely, radiation-hard.
Verdict
Your commissioning sample, unchanged. It validates the whole acquisition chain — drift, tilt reproducibility, delay-axis stability — against a system where the right answer is already known.

Nanocrystal acoustic breathing modes

Au, Ag, Bi₂Se₃, CsPbBr₃ — or the same VO₂ nanobeams
Bragg peak displacement 295 K native Dispersed NCs Risk: very low
Why 3D
The expansion is anisotropic. A rotation series returns the full transient strain tensor εij, where one zone axis returns one or two components and a guess at the rest.
Timing
Period scales with size: roughly 10–100 ps for 20–100 nm particles. An almost perfect match to a 2 ps response and a sub-nanosecond delay range — several full oscillations inside your window.
RT impact
None. Elastic constants shift slightly with temperature, which is if anything a useful cross-check once you have a heating holder.
Bonus
The period is set by size and elastic constants, so a size series gives an internal calibration of your own delay axis — cheap insurance against a systematic timing error.
Verdict
Second commissioning sample and a clean standalone result. Very hard for this one to fail.

If you only pick four

A sequence, not a ranking

Commission on something that cannot fail, take one guaranteed result, then spend the risk budget once on each of the two arguments that justify the instrument.

First · commission

Si or Ge membrane, then nanocrystal breathing modes

Ambient-native, zero fatigue, known answer. Exercises every part of the 4D chain, and the breathing-mode period doubles as an independent check on your delay-axis calibration.

Second · guaranteed result

VO₂ nanobeams, poised at 325–335 K

Large unambiguous signal at a fluence the sample survives, and a real 3D question waiting at the end of it — which monoclinic phase the transient state actually is. Also teaches you what the heating holder does to drift and eucentricity.

Third · physics paper

TbTe₃ first, then 1T-TaS₂

The tritelluride is the better first CDW experiment — ambient-native, simpler, well-benchmarked. Take TaS₂ second, at ambient in its NC phase or poised at 340 K for the NC → IC transition, where the physics is literally in the L direction.

Fourth · method paper

Fe(II)-triazole spin-crossover nanoparticles

"We refined a transient molecular structure from a single nanocrystal" is still the sentence that sells U-3DED. Riskier without the cryogenic benchmark, so attempt it once the first three have told you your real drift and dose budget.

Acquisition notes

The first three are specific to running at ambient
  • Get a heating holder before you get anything else. It converts the room-temperature constraint from a restriction into a control parameter, and poising 5–15 K below a transition is what makes VO₂, TaS₂, BaTiO₃ and the spin-crossover work give large signals at safe fluence. Nothing else on this list changes your results as much per unit cost.
  • Steady-state heating is now your dominant systematic. Without a cryostat there is no thermal ballast — the only heat path is the support film and the grid. Keep the repetition rate low (10–50 kHz for oxides on carbon), prefer graphene or thin SiN over amorphous carbon, and compare the t < 0 lattice parameter against a genuine pump-off measurement at every working point. Poised near a transition this is critical: average power can carry you across it without any sign in the data.
  • Assume 3–10× less dose headroom on soft materials. That factor is what cryo cooling was buying you on perovskites, MOFs, spin-crossover compounds and molecular crystals. Dose-fractionate across many crystallites and merge, rather than pushing one crystal through the full delay series.
  • Scan delay innermost. At each tilt angle, step the full delay series before moving the stage, so slow drift affects every delay equally at a given tilt instead of being aliased into the time axis. If you must scan angle innermost, bracket each series with repeated reference delays.
  • Put a negative-delay frame at every tilt. This is what makes the weak-superlattice experiments — Bi-manganites, spin crossover, orbital order — possible at all. Per-tilt normalisation removes the drift that would otherwise swamp a 1% intensity change.
  • Beam-tilt integration matters more here than in static 3DED. Your BEAM_TILT_STEP and BEAM_TILT_RANGE parameters improve completeness and damp dynamical effects — the difference between tracking intensities and actually refining a transient structure.
